NEET-PG 2025 counselling · Cut-off revision of 13 January 2026

The floor fell out

To fill roughly 20,000 empty postgraduate medical seats, India cut the NEET-PG qualifying bar to the 7th percentile for general candidates and to zero for SC, ST and OBC candidates. Nearly 96,000 more doctors instantly became eligible. The seats got filled. The argument is over who the filling was really for.

What actually changed

One notification moved the bar by four-fifths

NBEMS revised the qualifying percentile for Round 3 of counselling. The general-category threshold fell from 276 marks to 103. For reserved categories it fell below zero — a candidate could finish with a negative score and still qualify.

The problem it was meant to solve

Nearly a third of the central pool was sitting empty

After two rounds of All India Quota counselling, thousands of MD and MS seats had no takers. Counting DNB and state-quota seats, the government put the national shortfall at close to 20,000.

All India Quota seats offered for counselling — 29,476

Filled after two rounds — 19,855 (67.4%) Vacant — 9,621 (32.6%)

Of those 9,621 empty AIQ seats, 5,213 were in government colleges (including DNB seats) — which is precisely why the "who benefits" question is contested. See below.

For the overwhelming majority of the 96,000 newly eligible doctors, the cut-off drop changed nothing. It made them eligible for seats they could not afford or did not want.

The contested part

Who the empty seats actually belonged to

The headline claim is that private colleges gained more than government ones. The evidence pulls in two directions, and both directions are worth seeing.

The case for private colleges gaining

The vacancies were built by private expansion

PG capacity in private institutions grew from roughly 36,000 to nearly 64,000 seats without matching demand — a surplus that had to be filled somehow. Deemed universities, almost all private and the most expensive tier, carry the heaviest concentration of unfilled seats per institution. In several state matrices going into Round 3, more than 90% of what was left on the table sat in private colleges. Seats that stay empty earn a private college nothing; a lower bar converts them into fee-paying students.

The case that government colleges gained too

More than half the central vacancies were public

Of the 9,621 AIQ seats lying vacant after Round 2, 5,213 were in government colleges and DNB institutions — a clear majority. Deemed universities' aggregate share of vacancies was slightly lower than that of state colleges, the largest cohort in central counselling. Government institutes were set to gain alongside private ones, and by the widely reported cases, they did: low scorers entered public medical colleges too.

How to hold both: government colleges held the larger raw block of vacancies inside the central pool, while private and deemed institutions dominate the seat matrix overall — and are the tier whose finances depend on filling seats. The disagreement is largely about which denominator you use, which is why the same episode reads as a rescue of public capacity in one account and a bailout of private capacity in another.

The fight over it

Three positions, one Supreme Court bench

WhoPosition
The CentreThe reduction did not alter merit — it only expanded the eligible pool. Ranks and the order of selection were untouched; 95,913 more candidates simply entered the queue.
NBEMSThe decision to cut the percentile came from the Union Health Ministry, not from the examining body, it told the Supreme Court.
Medical educatorsWithout adequate faculty, reliable exit examinations and real clinical exposure, lowering the entry bar risks degrading specialist training — and, downstream, patient care and public trust.

The through-line

A supply problem answered with an eligibility fix

Empty PG seats are mostly a price and placement problem: high private fees, unloved non-clinical branches, and colleges in towns candidates don't want. Lowering the qualifying score does not make a seat cheaper or a branch more attractive — it only enlarges the set of people permitted to buy in. That is why 96,000 became eligible and roughly 2,000 of them actually converted.
